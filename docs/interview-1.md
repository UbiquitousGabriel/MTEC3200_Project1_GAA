# Interview Notes

**Participant:** Meg

**Date:** September 15, 2026

## Questions & Answers

**Q1: [What got you into religion?]**
A: "I grew up in a Christian household, so getting into religion just felt natural. I grew up going to church every Sunday morning and night and Bible study on Wednesday nights."

**Q2: [What does ideal religious participation look like to you compared to reality?]**
A: "Ideal religious participation looks a little bit different for everyone. Christians are encouraged in the Bible to fellowship with other believers, but it's not as simple as 'every Sunday morning'. Religious participation doesn't even have to look like going to church; church isn't the building that Christianity takes place in, it's the body of believers. For me, ideal participation looks like going to church to study and worship on Sunday mornings, partaking in personal study daily at home, and fellowshipping with other believers during the week. Some weeks I may feel like going to church on Sunday morning and Sunday night."

**Q3: [How do you maintain the balance of a social life and religious activites?]**
A: "I think realistically social life and religious activities are integrated seamlessly for me. I enjoy spending time with the people I go to church with and talking with them before and after study and services. I spend time with people who are not Christians and consider that to be a very healthy part of my social life as well. Going to church does not keep me from any other social activities. Maybe I don't stay up as late on Saturday nights because I know I have church on Sunday, but that's about it. Going to church does rank above social events on my calendar; I don't schedule hangouts or extra activities during church on Sundays. If I went to a church with services on Wednesday nights that I attended, I wouldn't schedule events on that night either."

**Q4: [If Sunday morning services didn't exist, how else would you want to practice your religion?]**
A: "One can have a healthy relationship with God without ever stepping foot in the building of a church. However, we are told that fellowshipping with other believers is important for our spiritual wellbeing. If Sunday morning services didn't exist, I would find time in other parts of the week to study with other believers and worship. I would still worship privately on Sunday, as New Testament Christians believe the day we know as Sunday is our commanded Sabbath, where we are to rest and spend time with God."

**Q5: [If you managed to go to a service, how do you feel after?]**
A: "I feel fulfilled after going to church services. I attend a home church currently, so our services look a little bit different from mainstream services, but I enjoy every part. I like praying with the other believers and sharing our burdens and praises. I love singing hymns and praising God in song. Our service follows more of a study format than a sermon, and I often get a lot out of the deep study of the Bible. Some weeks I get more out of study than others. Some weeks, a study will really speak to me and feel like something I needed to hear or be reminded of. Generally, on the way home from church, my husband and I will talk about the study and what we found interesting/what we got out of it. Sunday service feels like a refresh; a reset to put me in the right Christian worldview/headspace for the upcoming week."

**Q6: [What does your typical Saturday night to Sunday morning routine look like?]**
A: "My Saturday evenings vary greatly for what I do. Sometimes we're out with friends bowling and having dinner and sometimes we have a quiet evening in. Either way, I make sure to go to bed at a reasonable hour so I am well rested for Sunday morning. Our Sunday morning service starts at 10:00AM and we leave the house at 9:45AM to drive there. We usually aim to wake up between 7AM and 8AM. It's usually a quiet lazy morning where I make breakfast and we (my husband and I) chat. We get cleaned up and dressed for church and then have some downtime before we leave. Usually, I'm working on getting into the right mindset for church, praying for church prayer requests. I usually also pray that God will open my heart to whatever it is we're studying that day so that His Word will make an impact on my heart."

## Synthesis

* **One surprising quote:** "Church isn't the building that Christianity takes place in, it's the body of believers."
* **One thing you didn't expect:** How seamlessly non-Christian social connections are balanced alongside religious commitments, without feeling conflict or exclusion.
* **What this makes you think about your problem:** Religious practice is deeply personalized and decoupled from traditional church infrastructure; solutions should focus on enabling community fellowship, spiritual mindset, and private routines rather than just institutional attendance.
