## Questions & Answers

**Q1: [What got you into religion?]**
A: "Over the course of my childhood I occasionally went to a Catholic church, as my mother's dad was (and remains to be) a deacon there. I participated in a lot of Christian activities growing up, including attending a Methodist church camp called Glenlake. My dedication to the faith wavered a lot in junior high and high school. When I was 16 I met the person who would later become my wife, and she invited me to attend the Baptist church that she and her family attended. This was around the beginning of when I began to really take my faith seriously and commit to what I believe."

**Q2: [What does ideal religious participation look like to you compared to reality?]**
A: "Ideal religious participation would be going to church or gathering with fellow believers at least once a week, reading the Bible regularly, taking communion with fellow believers regularly, and participating in religious activities with other Christians outside of church hours. In reality I attend a home church nearly every Sunday, I read the bible far less than I believe I should, we recieve Communion less often than I'd like but still often enough, and at the moment we do not participate in any religious activities outside of going to church."

**Q3: [How do you maintain the balance of a social life and religious activities?]**
A: "I have found that putting God first doesn't take away from my social life in any significant way. If anything it seems to add to it. Granted there are plenty of activities that I should not participate in because of my faith, but I never participated in such activities to begin with. Not everything I do is related to my faith of course, nor is everyone that's a part of my social life a Christian, but I think my beliefs are known well enough to those in each social circle I am a part of. I have not had to compromise a social life for religious activities (or vice versa) so far."

**Q4: [If Sunday morning services didn't exist, how else would you want to practice your religion?]**
A: "That depends on what is meant by Sunday morning services. If I couldn't go to church in the morning, I'd go in the evening or on a different day of the week, such as Saturday or Wednesday which are other, less common, days where one could go to church. If services in general ceased, or if church buildings disappeared entirely, I'd be doing what I am now; meeting with a group of fellow believers at the home of our pastor. Where, when, and on what day the service happens doesn't much matter."

**Q5: [If you managed to go to a service, how do you feel after?]**
A: "It depends on the service, but more often than not I feel spiritually refreshed."

**Q6: [What does your typical Saturday night to Sunday morning routine look like?]**
A: "Saturday evenings tend to be one of two things. Either a quiet evening in, relaxing from the work of the week, or my wife and I are out with friends usually bowling or going to dinner. In either case, we are home by about 10PM to get enough rest for Sunday. On Sunday morning, my wife and I wake up between 7 and 8 (she tends to wake up before me). We eat breakfast, hang out around the house for a bit, and sometimes play some videogames before getting ready to leave for church. Church for us starts at a bit past 10AM and is about a 15 minute drive away from our home. When we arrive at our pastor's house we spend some time visiting with the other people present. As for service we start with prayer requests, then we sing hymns, then our pastor begins the lesson he's prepared. By around 11:45 the service has concluded and we, my wife and I, either stop somewhere for lunch or we head straight home where we tend to spend the rest of the day."

## Synthesis

* **One surprising quote:** "Putting God first doesn't take away from my social life in any significant way. If anything it seems to add to it."
* **One thing you didn't expect:** Playing video games during downtime on Sunday morning as part of the pre-church routine.
* **What this makes you think about your problem:** Religious practice is deeply adaptable to location and schedule; participants value community gathering and spiritual refreshment regardless of whether it happens in a traditional church building or a private home.
