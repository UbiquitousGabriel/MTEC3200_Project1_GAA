Synthesis:

Across the interviews, the participants who kept showing up with friction were young, active Christians who balance everyday social lives while striving for personal spiritual discipline outside of official church walls. When friction happens—such as falling short on personal Bible reading, lacking mid-week Christian community, or adjusting when Sunday services are missed—they do not abandon their faith; instead, they pivot to private prayer routines at home, attend intimate home church gatherings, or discuss morning studies with their spouse on the drive home. The root cause of this gap is that while institutional Sunday services provide structure, personal habits like independent daily scripture reading lack accountability and integrated daily routine. What surprised me is that neither participant views traditional church buildings as necessary for true religious practice, seamlessly integrating their secular social lives and Saturday night entertainment without feeling that their faith limits them. The real problem is that while individuals feel spiritual fulfillment from communal fellowship, they struggle to sustain structured, solo spiritual habits throughout the week without the built-in momentum of a group.
