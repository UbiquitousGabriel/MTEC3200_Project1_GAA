> “When great souls die,  
> the air around us becomes light, rare, sterile.  
> We breathe, briefly.  
> Our eyes, briefly,  
> see with  
> a hurtful clarity.”  
> –Maya Angelou, in “When Great Trees Fall”

I became a death doula to companion people in their grief. That was the calling I trained for. I did not expect how many calls would come on behalf of a dog, a cat, a horse, an iguana.

The first time someone called about a dying cat, I almost said that wasn’t my work. I caught myself. She was not asking me to save the cat. She was asking me to help her hold what was coming, the same way I would help her hold the death of a parent.

So I said yes. And I kept saying yes.

Companion animal grief gets little room in our culture. People apologize before they finish describing their loss. They say “I know it’s just a dog” or “I feel silly grieving this much.” They believe that this love counts less; that this grief should stay small and be managed alone.

I’ve sat with a man who could not wash the blanket his dog died on, the smell his last thread to twelve years of companionship. I’ve held space for someone grieving an iguana, an animal most people would not think to grieve at all. That grief moved through her the way grief moves through anyone who has lost a being who relied on them completely and loved them without condition.

Grief does not measure itself by species. It measures itself by the bond that has been broken. A companion animal knows us unguarded and unwell, and chooses to stay with us through it all. When that attachment ends, the absence is not smaller because the body was smaller.

We’re rarely taught to anticipate losing an animal we love, even though most of us who share our lives with them will witness their death. There is no cultural script, no permission slip that says this loss deserves full mourning. People improvise alone, apologizing for tears at their desk.

This ministry allows me to hand people that permission slip: their grief is not too much, not silly, not something to minimize. I help them build a farewell ceremony, write a memorial blessing, or sit with a dying animal so neither faces that threshold alone. Every loss I have witnessed–fur, feathers, or scales–has been real, and worthy of honor and care.

## Prayer

Spirit of all that lives and breathes, hold the ones who grieve a companion today. Let their love be counted whole, their tears be counted holy, and their heart be given room enough to break and mend in its own time. Amen.
